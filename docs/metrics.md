# Core Business Metrics (CAC, LTV, Take Rate)

A beginner-friendly guide to understanding the essential metrics that drive marketplace startups.

---

## Introduction

Every successful startup needs to track the right numbers. For a tiffin/meal service marketplace like BellyBento, three metrics stand out as the most important:

- **CAC** – How much it costs to get a new customer
- **LTV** – How much money a customer brings over their lifetime
- **Take Rate** – How much the platform earns from each transaction

These metrics help founders make smart decisions and show investors that the business can grow profitably.

> **Simple Rule:** A healthy business makes more from customers (LTV) than it spends to acquire them (CAC).

---

## CAC (Customer Acquisition Cost)

### What is CAC?

CAC tells you how much money you spend to get one new customer. This includes all marketing expenses like ads, promotions, referral bonuses, and sales team costs.

### Formula

```
CAC = Total Marketing Spend / Number of Customers Acquired
```

### Example

Your startup spends ₹50,000 on Instagram ads and Google Ads in one month. From this campaign, 100 new customers sign up for tiffin subscriptions.

```
CAC = ₹50,000 / 100 customers = ₹500 per customer
```

### Key Insights

- **Lower CAC = Better** – The less you spend to acquire a customer, the more profit you keep
- **CAC varies by channel** – Word-of-mouth and referrals usually have the lowest CAC
- **Track CAC by source** – Know which marketing channels give you the best return

---

## LTV (Lifetime Value)

### What is LTV?

LTV (Lifetime Value) tells you the total revenue a customer generates during their entire relationship with your platform. It answers: "How much is one customer worth to us?"

### Formula

```
LTV = Revenue per Customer per Month × Number of Months Customer Stays
```

Or more simply:

```
LTV = Average Order Value × Orders per Month × Customer Lifetime (in months)
```

### Example (Tiffin Service)

A customer orders a monthly tiffin plan worth ₹3,000 per month. On average, customers stay subscribed for 8 months before churning.

```
LTV = ₹3,000 × 8 months = ₹24,000
```

This customer is worth ₹24,000 to your business over their lifetime.

### Key Insights

- **Retention is everything** – Keeping customers longer dramatically increases LTV
- **Small improvements compound** – Increasing retention by just 1 month can significantly boost LTV
- **Quality matters** – Happy customers stay longer and refer others

## Take Rate

### What is Take Rate?

Take rate is the percentage of each transaction that the platform keeps as revenue. It's how marketplaces make money – by taking a small cut of every order.

### Formula

```
Take Rate = (Platform Earnings / Total Transaction Value) × 100
```

### Example

A customer orders ₹3,000 worth of tiffins through BellyBento. The platform charges a 10% commission.

```
Platform Earnings = ₹3,000 × 10% = ₹300
Take Rate = (₹300 / ₹3,000) × 100 = 10%
```

The provider gets ₹2,700, and the platform keeps ₹300.

### Why Take Rate Matters

- **Revenue model** – This is how the marketplace generates income
- **Balance is key** – Too high, and providers leave. Too low, and the platform isn't sustainable.
- **Industry benchmarks:**
  - Food delivery apps: 15-30%
  - Tiffin marketplaces: 8-15%
  - E-commerce: 10-20%

### BellyBento Commission Structure (5-10%)

We use a tiered commission model based on the provider's existing customer base:

| Provider Tier | Existing Customers | Commission % | Monthly Example (₹3,000/customer) |
|---------------|-------------------|--------------|-----------------------------------|
| **Starter** | 1 - 15 | 5% | ₹150 per customer |
| **Growing** | 16 - 30 | 6% | ₹180 per customer |
| **Established** | 31 - 50 | 7% | ₹210 per customer |
| **Professional** | 51 - 75 | 8% | ₹240 per customer |
| **Expert** | 76 - 100 | 9% | ₹270 per customer |
| **Premium** | 100+ | 10% | ₹300 per customer |

#### Why This Structure?

| Charge Less to Small Providers (5-6%) | Charge More to Large Providers (9-10%) |
|---------------------------------------|----------------------------------------|
| Help them grow and survive | They get more value from platform |
| Build loyalty early | Higher volume = can afford commission |
| Investment in future top sellers | More platform resources used |
| Lower risk of them leaving | Strong dependency on platform |

#### Example Calculation

**Small Provider (20 customers):**
```
Monthly Revenue = 20 × ₹3,000 = ₹60,000
Commission (6%) = ₹3,600
Provider Keeps = ₹56,400
```

**Large Provider (120 customers):**
```
Monthly Revenue = 120 × ₹3,000 = ₹3,60,000
Commission (10%) = ₹36,000
Provider Keeps = ₹3,24,000
```

> **Note:** Both providers are profitable. The large provider pays more in absolute terms but earns significantly more.

## Relationship Between CAC, LTV, and Take Rate

These three metrics are deeply connected. Here's how they work together:

### The Golden Rule

```
LTV should be at least 3× higher than CAC
```

If you spend ₹500 to acquire a customer (CAC), that customer should bring in at least ₹1,500 in revenue (LTV).

### How Take Rate Affects LTV

Your actual earnings from a customer depend on take rate:

```
Platform LTV = Customer Spend × Take Rate × Customer Lifetime
```

### Good vs Bad Scenario Comparison

| Metric | ❌ Struggling Business | ✅ Healthy Business |
|--------|----------------------|-------------------|
| CAC | ₹1,000 | ₹500 |
| Customer Monthly Spend | ₹2,000 | ₹3,000 |
| Take Rate | 8% | 12% |
| Customer Lifetime | 3 months | 8 months |
| Platform LTV | ₹480 | ₹2,880 |
| LTV:CAC Ratio | 0.48:1 ❌ | 5.76:1 ✅ |

The struggling business loses money on every customer. The healthy business makes nearly 6× what it spends to acquire them.

## Example Calculation (Tiffin Startup Scenario)

Let's walk through a complete example for a tiffin marketplace like BellyBento.

### Given Data

| Parameter | Value |
|-----------|-------|
| Average meal price | ₹100 |
| Meals per day | 1 (lunch) |
| Days per month | 30 |
| Monthly customer spend | ₹3,000 |
| Take rate | 10% |
| Average customer lifetime | 6 months |
| Monthly marketing budget | ₹25,000 |
| New customers acquired | 50 |

### Step 1: Calculate CAC

```
CAC = Marketing Spend / Customers Acquired
CAC = ₹25,000 / 50
CAC = ₹500 per customer
```

### Step 2: Calculate Platform Revenue per Customer per Month

```
Monthly Platform Revenue = Customer Spend × Take Rate
Monthly Platform Revenue = ₹3,000 × 10%
Monthly Platform Revenue = ₹300 per customer per month
```

### Step 3: Calculate Platform LTV

```
Platform LTV = Monthly Platform Revenue × Customer Lifetime
Platform LTV = ₹300 × 6 months
Platform LTV = ₹1,800 per customer
```

### Step 4: Calculate LTV:CAC Ratio

```
LTV:CAC Ratio = ₹1,800 / ₹500 = 3.6:1 ✅
```

### Analysis

| Metric | Value | Status |
|--------|-------|--------|
| CAC | ₹500 | Good |
| Platform LTV | ₹1,800 | Healthy |
| LTV:CAC Ratio | 3.6:1 | ✅ Above 3:1 threshold |
| Gross Profit per Customer | ₹1,300 | Positive |

This is a healthy business! For every ₹500 spent on acquisition, the platform earns ₹1,800 over the customer's lifetime.

## Key Takeaways

### For Founders

- ✅ **Track CAC by channel** – Know where your best customers come from
- ✅ **Focus on retention** – Increasing customer lifetime is often easier than lowering CAC
- ✅ **Aim for LTV:CAC > 3:1** – This is the benchmark for a sustainable business
- ✅ **Find the right take rate** – Balance platform revenue with provider satisfaction
- ✅ **Reduce CAC over time** – Build referral programs and organic growth channels

### For Investors

- 📊 LTV:CAC ratio shows unit economics health
- 📊 Take rate indicates pricing power and market position
- 📊 Improving retention signals product-market fit
- 📊 Declining CAC over time shows brand strength

### Quick Reference

| Metric | Formula | Good Benchmark |
|--------|---------|----------------|
| CAC | Marketing Spend ÷ Customers | Lower is better |
| LTV | Monthly Revenue × Lifetime | Higher is better |
| Take Rate | Platform Earnings ÷ GMV × 100 | 10-15% for tiffin |
| LTV:CAC | LTV ÷ CAC | > 3:1 |

---

## Glossary

- **GMV (Gross Merchandise Value)** – Total value of all transactions on the platform
- **Churn** – When customers stop using the service
- **Retention** – Keeping customers active over time
- **Unit Economics** – Profit/loss analysis per customer or transaction
